    1. E-01
      Brushless DC motor
      Status
      Complete

      In the graph ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A hand-wound outrunner, designed for a torque density not available off the shelf at this diameter. A gearbox then being designed needed more continuous torque inside its envelope than hobby outrunners of that size give: they are wound for RPM, not torque. The choice was a larger motor with a heavier reduction, or a lower-Kv winding that keeps the package. This motor was built to measure that trade-off directly.

      Goals
      • More torque at this diameter than an off-the-shelf hobby outrunner, from a lower-Kv winding in the same package.
      • Built to measure the trade-off directly: more turns lower Kv and raise torque per amp, but cost slot area, phase resistance and copper loss.

    2. —
      PCB Motor
      Status
      In progress

      In the graph ↑

      Details
      Summary

      An axial-flux, coreless motor whose stator is the copper in a multilayer PCB, with a Halbach or back-iron magnet rotor. The physics is honest but unforgiving (thin copper, no iron, poor thermal path), and the two things that make it useful are radius (Km ∝ r², loss ∝ 1/r⁴) and running the driver at ≥ 100 kHz to tame the ripple in µH-scale windings. This is the motor on its own; the drone that uses it is the PCB Drone.

      Goals
      • A 25–40 mm stator on a 6–8 layer, 2 oz board that spins a rotor with measured Kt, Km and a thermal curve on the dyno.
      • Test both rotor styles: back-iron disc and Halbach array (Halbach is the one the drone needs).
      • Characterize copper loss vs. PWM frequency with the FOC board to settle the 100 kHz question with data.
      • A rim-drive variant (annular coil band at large radius, cut-out center) as the second board, since that is the geometry the drone wants.
      Unlocks
      PCB Drone

    3. C-02
      Ultra-Mobility Vehicle, scaled
      Status
      In progress

      In the graph ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A ~3.4 kg two-wheeled acrobatic bicycle robot scaled from the RAI Institute's UMV (s = 0.525, 0.21 m Neck). It drives, balances, jumps, and is designed toward front and back flips as the headline capability. Five actuated DOFs: a cable-driven bevel differential (S1/S2 → virtual µ pitch and φ out-of-plane), Head–Neck pitch (qh), steering (qs), rear drive (qw). RobStride quasi-direct-drive actuators on one CAN bus, Raspberry Pi 5 running a 50 Hz RL policy that outputs joint PD targets.

      This is the flagship and the root of the dynamic-robot line: it needs nothing else on the tree, and it produces the RL sim-to-real pipeline (URDF export → IsaacLab training → Pi deployment) that the Wheel-Leg reuses.

      Goals
      • Balance and drive at ≥ 3.8 m/s (steering-only balance, 156 ms tipping constant).
      • Jump 0.40 m at the CoM (takeoff 2.80 m/s, ~2.7× BW ground reaction, ~250 W peak at the CoM).
      • Land partial-stroke at up to ~4× BW without damage (cables, anchors, bearings sized to it).
      • Front flip and back flip (0.58 s flight, ~23 rad/s peak pitch rate, ~2.2:1 inertia modulation by tucking).
      • A reusable sim-to-real pipeline: URDF, IsaacLab environment, domain randomization, policy export, on-robot runner.
      Unlocks
      Wheel-Leg (Beni-class)

      Bokser et al., System Design of the Ultra Mobility Vehicle (arXiv 2602.22118) · RAI Institute

    2. —
      PCB Drone
      Status
      Planned

      Locked: needs PCB MotorIn the graph ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A whoop-sized quad where one PCB is the frame, the four stators, the ESCs and the flight controller — plus four printed hubs and props. Two motor geometries are candidates: a hub stator under a Halbach prop hub (simpler; marginal at 16–18 mm stator), and a rim drive with magnets on the prop tips and an annular coil band around each duct opening (much better copper loss at large radius; centrifugal retention and rotor inertia are the problems). Physics is marginal, which is exactly why the thrust-stand gate exists. If it flies, it is something that does not exist yet.

      Goals
      • Thrust-stand gate: ≥ 12–15 g thrust per motor, added rotor + copper mass ≤ 3 g, stator ≤ 80 °C after 3 min at hover thrust, ≥ 2 g/W at hover.
      • 75–85 mm wheelbase, 40–45 mm props, 1S 450 mAh or 2S 300 mAh, 30–40 g all-up.
      • ESC: four AM32-style channels on the board (or the FOC board's 100 kHz mode); bidirectional DShot for RPM filtering.
      • Flies like a whoop, not just hovers: ~4 × 15–20 g at full throttle.
      • TPU ducts bolt on as bumpers and magnet shields; soft-mounted IMU island.
      Needs
      PCB Motor
    3. —
      Wheel-Leg (Beni-class)
      Status
      Planned

      Locked: needs Ultra-Mobility Vehicle, scaled and QDD Actuator ModuleIn the graph ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A small, fast, springy two-wheeled-leg balancer in the class of Beni (≈ 1.75 kg, 29 km/h, 25 cm jump, stairs, self-righting) — explicitly not Handle: no arm, no box. Four home-built QDD actuators in the legs, two direct-drive wheel motors, an IMU, and the RL pipeline from the UMV. What sets it apart from a Beni clone is the performance level: real speed on bigger wheels, lean-in cornering and holding a drift, fast stairs down without stopping, self-righting from any orientation, and a stabilized camera head for the vision layer later. The jump is a design decision, not the point.

      Goals
      • ≈ 1.5–2.5 kg, ≥ 40 km/h on the flat, balances at rest, survives a shove from any direction at speed.
      • Stairs: down a full flight at speed without stopping; up a standard flight.
      • Self-rights from any orientation (the tail, if fitted, is the lever for this).
      • Jump, by design tier: torque-only ≈ 30–40 cm, parallel-elastic ≈ 50–60 cm, latched ≈ 1 m.
      • Camera head (2-axis) for tracking a person or the drift car at speed, added later.
      Needs
      Ultra-Mobility Vehicle, scaled and QDD Actuator Module
