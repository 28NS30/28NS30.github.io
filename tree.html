      A hand-wound outrunner, designed for a torque density not available off the shelf at this diameter. A gearbox then being designed needed more continuous torque inside its envelope than hobby outrunners of that size give: they are wound for RPM, not torque. The choice was a larger motor with a heavier reduction, or a lower-Kv winding that keeps the package. This motor was built to measure that trade-off directly.

      Goals

    2. pcb-motor

      PCB Motor

      Tier
      0
      Status
      In progress
      Complexity
      H2 S2 E4
      Unlocks
      1

      In progressIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      An axial-flux, coreless motor whose stator is the copper in a multilayer PCB, with a Halbach or back-iron magnet rotor. Already explored in earlier chats: the physics is honest but unforgiving (thin copper, no iron, poor thermal path), and the two things that make it useful are radius (Km ∝ r², loss ∝ 1/r⁴) and running the driver at ≥ 100 kHz to tame the ripple in µH-scale windings. This node is the motor on its own; the drone that uses it is pcb-drone.

      Goals
      • A 25–40 mm stator on a 6–8 layer, 2 oz board that spins a rotor with measured Kt, Km and a thermal curve on the dyno.
      • Test both rotor styles: back-iron disc and Halbach array (Halbach is the one the drone needs).
      • Characterize copper loss vs. PWM frequency with the FOC board to settle the 100 kHz question with data.
      • A rim-drive variant (annular coil band at large radius, cut-out center) as the second board, since that is the geometry the drone wants.
      Unlocks
      PCB Drone

      Carl Bugeja's PCB motors (reference)

      FOC Driver Board

      Tier
      0

      Ready: needs nothingIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      The motor driver everything else runs on: a field-oriented-control board with current sensing, encoder input, CAN, and PWM fast enough for the low-inductance windings of the PCB motor and linear motor. Two form factors from one design: a multi-channel board (4-in-1 or 6-in-1) for the drift car and motion platform, and a single-channel puck that fits inside the QDD actuator housing. Not exciting on its own; nine projects sit on it.

      Goals
      • Sinusoidal FOC with dq current control, ≥ 20 kHz current loop, PWM selectable up to ≥ 100 kHz for coreless/ironless windings.
      • Per-channel: ≥ 30 A peak, 24–48 V bus, phase current sensing (inline or low-side shunts), magnetic encoder (AS5047P SPI / ABI) and hall input.
      • CAN (classic, 1 Mbps) with a RobStride-style MIT-mode command set so the UMV's runner code works on both.
      • Multi-channel board: 4 or 6 channels, one MCU per channel or one fast MCU with hardware timers (decide at rev 0).
      • Puck: ≤ 40 mm diameter, fits the QDD housing, temperature sensing, bus-side bulk capacitance.

    7. dyno

      Motor Dynamometer

      Tier
      0
      Status
      Planned
      Complexity
      H2 S2 E3
      Unlocks
      2

      Ready: needs nothingIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A bench that measures what a motor actually does: torque, speed, phase current, voltage, input power, and stator temperature over time. From those it produces Kt, Kv, Km, efficiency maps, and thermal limits. It is what makes every motor project on the tree credible, and it is how V3 gets compared against the coreless PCB motor with numbers instead of feelings. Build it before V3 is finished so V3 gets measured properly.

      Goals
      • Torque 0–5 N·m (reaction-arm on a load cell, or a small torque cell), speed 0–10k rpm, up to ~1 kW electrical.
      • Load: a second motor in regen/brake mode (cheap, controllable) or a hysteresis brake (smoother at low speed).
      • Logs at ≥ 100 Hz to CSV; a script turns a sweep into Kt, Kv, Km = Kt/√R, efficiency map, and a thermal time constant.
      • Repeatable mounting for outrunners, inrunners, and the QDD module.
      Unlocks
      QDD Actuator Module, V3 BLDC Motor (laminated stator)
    8. C-02

      UMV — Ultra Mobility Vehicle

      Tier
      0
      Status
      Planned
      Complexity
      H5 S5 E4
      Unlocks
      1

      Ready: needs nothingIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A ~3.4 kg two-wheeled acrobatic bicycle robot scaled from the RAI Institute's UMV (s = 0.525, 0.21 m Neck). It drives, balances, jumps, and is designed toward front and back flips as the headline capability. Five actuated DOFs: a cable-driven bevel differential (S1/S2 → virtual µ pitch and φ out-of-plane), Head–Neck pitch (qh), steering (qs), rear drive (qw). RobStride quasi-direct-drive actuators on one CAN bus, Raspberry Pi 5 running a 50 Hz RL policy that outputs joint PD targets. The full spec is in spec.md.

      This is the flagship and the root of the dynamic-robot line: it needs nothing else on the tree, and it produces the RL sim-to-real pipeline (URDF export → IsaacLab training → Pi deployment) that wheel-leg and inline-wheel-leg reuse.

      Goals
      • Balance and drive at ≥ 3.8 m/s (steering-only balance, 156 ms tipping constant).
      • Jump 0.40 m at the CoM (takeoff 2.80 m/s, ~2.7× BW ground reaction, ~250 W peak at the CoM).
      • Land partial-stroke at up to ~4× BW without damage (cables, anchors, bearings sized to it).
      • Front flip and back flip (0.58 s flight, ~23 rad/s peak pitch rate, ~2.2:1 inertia modulation by tucking).
      • A reusable sim-to-real pipeline: URDF, IsaacLab environment, domain randomization, policy export, on-robot runner.

      Locked: needs PCB MotorIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A whoop-sized quad where one PCB is the frame, the four stators, the ESCs and the flight controller — plus four printed hubs and props. Two motor geometries are candidates: a hub stator under a Halbach prop hub (simpler; marginal at 16–18 mm stator), and a rim drive with magnets on the prop tips and an annular coil band around each duct opening (much better copper loss at large radius; centrifugal retention and rotor inertia are the problems). Physics is marginal, which is exactly why the thrust-stand gate exists and why feasibility is scored 2. If it flies, it is something that does not exist yet.

      Goals

      • ESC: four AM32-style channels on the board (or the FOC board's 100 kHz mode); bidirectional DShot for RPM filtering.
      • Flies like a whoop, not just hovers: ~4 × 15–20 g at full throttle.
      • TPU ducts bolt on as bumpers and magnet shields; soft-mounted IMU island.

      A direct-drive linear motor: a track of coils (PCB or wound) and a Halbach mover, or the reverse (magnet track, moving coil), commutated by the FOC board with a linear encoder. It is the single highest-leverage project on the tree: it gates the carrier catapult, the fast printer axes, and the drift car's active suspension, and those three are independent of each other once it exists.       • Commutation and position control on the FOC board (linear = the same dq math with an electrical angle from the scale).
      • Measured force constant, force ripple, and thermal limit.

    2. printer-5axis

      5-Axis Linear-Motor Printer

      Tier
      2
      Status
      Planned
      Complexity
      H5 S5 E3
      Unlocks
      —

      Locked: needs Linear MotorIn the drawing ↑

      Details
      Summary

      A 3D printer with linear-motor XY(Z) axes for speed and a two-axis trunnion bed (tilt + rotate) for axes 4 and 5. "Fast as hell" comes from the linear motors: no belts, no screws, 50k+ mm/s² accelerations and ~1 m/s travel, at which point the melt rate of the hotend is the limit. Five axes with a rotary bed enable the things that make multi-axis worth having: support-free 90° overhangs, conformal top layers, layers oriented to load, and printing onto existing parts. The mechanical build is finite; the slicer is half the project, because multi-axis toolpath generation is not a checkbox.

      Goals
      • Linear axes: ≥ 1 m/s, ≥ 50 m/s², positioning repeatability ≤ 20 µm.
      • Trunnion: ±90° tilt, continuous rotate, ≤ 0.05° repeatability, 200 mm bed.
      • High-flow hotend (≥ 40 mm³/s) with input shaping tuned for the new mass and stiffness.
      • Software: a working conformal/non-planar workflow (Open5x-style or own), with a demo part that is not printable on a 3-axis machine.

    A robot that rides a passive pogo stick the way a human does: no jumping actuator, the hop comes from a mass moving relative to the stick, pumped at the right phase of the spring. Deliberately mechanism-dense, per Neo: a shuttle mass on a linear axis for the pumping, the whole mass assembly on a two-axis pivot for lean steering, a reaction wheel inside the mass for yaw turns in the air, a tilting foot so the takeoff angle is not set by how the stick happens to land, and possibly an adjustable preload collar to tune hop height without changing the pump. The exact mechanism is still open; the philosophy is fixed.

    Goals
    • Sustained hopping from rest to a steady height by resonance pumping alone.
    • Steering and balance by lean (a ballbot on a spring): straight line, then a circle, then a 180° yaw in the air.
    • Recover from a mis-timed hop; land and stop on command (or fall gracefully into a cage).
    • Instrumented: spring compression, mass position, IMU; logs for the control write-up.
    Uses if built
    QDD Actuator Module
